IoT Intermedia

Modular embedded physical computing toolkit for intersensory and embodied immersion

Digital creation projects increasingly reinforce immersion by involving the human body and its environment, beyond conventional audiovisual content. Such works engage several senses at once and place bodily experience in space, which requires ad hoc systems that combine digital technologies with physical objects. That practice belongs to physical computing and the Internet of Things (IoT), yet it carries requirements that conventional IoT applications do not cover. IoT Intermedia answered that gap with a modular toolkit of embedded physical computing for intersensory and embodied interaction, developed at the Société des Arts Technologiques (SAT) between January 2024 and April 2025 with funding from the Programme de soutien aux organismes (PSO) of Quebec’s Ministère de l’Économie, de l’Innovation et de l’Énergie (MEIE).

Pia Baltazar directed the project and I served as technical lead, with Marek Blottière as project manager and a SAT team including Jean-Michaël Celerier, Manuel Bolduc, Charles Bicari, Olivier Gauthier, David Ledoux, and Vincent Brault. The creative partners each brought a use case: Compagnie Totem Contemporain, directed by Jean-François Laporte, with the SpiRitual project; Navid Navab with Organism; Erin Gee with BioSynth, a modular do it yourself instrument that uses emotional biofeedback as a control parameter for synthesized sound; and Compagnie Fheel Concepts, directed by Corinne Linder, with VIB.E-MOTION, which captures physiological indicators of emotion between performers and an audience. The academic and industrial network included Sofian Audry (Hexagram, Université du Québec à Montréal), Alice Jarry (Milieux, Concordia University), Marcelo M. Wanderley (Input Devices and Music Interaction Laboratory, McGill University), the Centre d’Innovation en Microélectronique du Québec (CIMEQ), and Wissem Maazoun (BusPas).

The project advanced in four phases. We first established a state of the art covering the literature on intersensoriality and embodiment alongside a technical review of IoT technologies, which set the conceptual and methodological framework. We then developed a base system for integrating and interconnecting external tools within a distributed computing environment, comprising hardware modules for sensor acquisition and actuator control, support libraries for data acquisition and preparation, and control and content creation tools. Furthermore, that base included a specialized operating system supporting real-time applications, compatible with the free authoring tools artists already use, exposing sensor and actuator connections directly through General Purpose Input Output (GPIO) and Pulse Width Modulation (PWM), and economical in memory and processor use.

The third phase developed software components for the specific functions the partner use cases required, among them gesture capture, haptic stimulation, and light emitting diode control embedded in connected garments; multichannel audio looping and processing inside an effects pedal; distributed actuator control for animating physical instruments and installations; embedded multichannel spatialized audio processing; distributed scenarization and orchestration; and computer vision with pose detection. Partners were trained on ossia and Puara so that they could carry the work into their own practice. The fourth phase produced operational demonstrators, with CIMEQ supporting their realization, intended for later maturation and transfer. The resulting toolkit, documented as the Espace Connecté pour l’Art et l’Internet des Objets (ECLO), gathers the software, the methodology, the process description, and a set of recommendations.
