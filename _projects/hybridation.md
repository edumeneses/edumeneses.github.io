Hybrid spaces that host artistic events, conferences, and networking activities remain an embryonic field of experimentation, and they raise open questions about how individuals are represented, how they interact with each other and with the environment, and how content is distributed. This research project, funded through the Programme de soutien aux organismes (PSO) of Quebec's Ministère de l'Économie, de l'Innovation et de l'Énergie (MEIE) and conducted with Moment Factory as partner, addressed those questions along three axes: the hybridization of physical and virtual spaces, collective interaction in the context of public installations, and the prototyping of digital installations.

The work built on tools created and maintained in the SAT laboratories. Scenic and its associated hardware station facilitate the deployment of telepresence experiences, while Satellite provides flexible virtual reality spaces on the Web. To these we added the results of a decade of research: Splash for video mapping, SATIE for audio spatialization, LivePose for pose and action estimation, and the Audiodice omnidirectional loudspeakers. Together they support installations that combine a virtual space with one or several physical spaces augmented by an interactive immersive scenography.

On the interaction axis, we favored equipping the space rather than the individuals, extending LivePose with automatic calibration of multiple depth or colour cameras, three dimensional pose coordinates derived from a calibrated camera set, improved action estimation, and deployment across networks of computers, including embedded ones. On the prototyping axis, the hypothesis was that creativity in new media, telepresence, and interactive immersion depends on interoperability between tools, ideally live and stream based. Consequently we worked on the shmdata library underlying switcher and Scenic, on the integration of libmapper and webmapper into our workflows, and on interoperability layers for external tools through shmdata, Network Device Interface (NDI), and other suitable protocols. Results were presented at the ACFAS congress in 2023.
