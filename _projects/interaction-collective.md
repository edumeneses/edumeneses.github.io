This research project aimed to make the creation of collective interaction experiences more accessible, treating collective interaction as any form of gestural interaction between humans in the physical world. The relevant behaviors range from contemporary dancers performing a carefully choreographed piece to a museum audience discovering an installation driven by the capture of their movements. The project was funded through the Programme de soutien aux organismes (PSO) of Quebec's Ministère de l'Économie, de l'Innovation et de l'Énergie (MEIE), and completed in 2025.

Our approach relied on non-intrusive capture technologies, which do not require participants to wear equipment on the body. We therefore targeted three research and development questions: which methodologies fuse real-time capture data into a single collective interaction experience; how to ensure the reliability of detection results from computer vision systems, including the adjustment of real-time processing parameters to the capture context; and how to develop high-level descriptive parameters that let detection results enter an ecosystem of software dedicated to interactive content.

The work mobilized expertise across the SAT innovation department in visual reconstruction, artificial intelligence for computer vision, and interoperability, with our pose and action estimation software LivePose as the main technical vehicle. Moreover, the project benefited from a collaboration with LAB7, whose mission is to host technological prototyping projects for the performing arts. That collaboration crystallized around three experimentation residencies on interactivity, which supplied the use cases we then analyzed.
