The Audiodice are dodecahedral omnidirectional loudspeakers developed at the Société des Arts Technologiques (SAT), together with the software ecosystem that drives them. In contrast with centralized spatialization systems, which assume a single idealized listening position, the Audiodice distribute divergent sound sources through a space, which makes them suited to collective and mobile listening in non-conventional venues.

The current technological maturation project takes the system from a functional prototype to a market-ready solution over twenty-four months. It pursues three objectives: optimizing the hardware design and the audio links for modularity, ease of deployment, and cost reduction, including portable conversion and power modules; developing and stabilizing a software stack for the creation, spatialization, and diffusion of volumetric audio content, extended to complementary devices such as the Satotem vertical diffusion system; and validating the commercial potential of the solution through experiments in real contexts with partners. The project is conducted with Jean-Michaël Celerier and David Ledoux, in collaboration with Paysages and Messier-Roy, and is funded through the Programme de soutien aux organismes (PSO) of Quebec's Ministère de l'Économie, de l'Innovation et de l'Énergie (MEIE).

A complementary grant from the Canada Foundation for Innovation (CFI), obtained with the Centre for Interdisciplinary Research in Music Media and Technology (CIRMMT), funds an Audiodice installation for research use, which extends the reach of the system beyond the SAT.
